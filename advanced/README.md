# Chapter 7: Advanced Probabilistic Models

Welcome to Chapter 7! In this capstone chapter, you will transition from deterministic optimization and discriminative modeling to **probabilistic generative models** and **latent-variable frameworks**. You will formulate models where observed data is generated by unobservable (hidden) underlying mechanisms, derive the **Expectation-Maximization (EM)** algorithm from first principles using Jensen's inequality, and implement dynamic programming algorithms on temporal sequence trellises.

## Numerical Stability in Probabilistic Modeling

Generative models multiply long chains of probabilities. In standard 64-bit floating point arithmetic, probabilities quickly underflow to zero:
$$P(o_1, \dots, o_T) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(o_t \mid \dots) \approx (0.1)^{100} = 10^{-100} \xrightarrow{\text{IEEE 754}} 0.0$$

To prevent catastrophic floating-point underflow, this chapter requires two standard numerical techniques:

### 1. The Log-Sum-Exp Trick
When computing the logarithm of a sum of exponentials (such as normalizing Gaussian mixture responsibilities or marginalizing state probabilities):
$$\ln \sum_{k=1}^K e^{z_k} = c + \ln \sum_{k=1}^K e^{z_k - c}, \quad \text{where } c = \max_{k} z_k$$
Subtracting the maximum value $c$ guarantees that the largest exponent is $e^0 = 1$, completely preventing floating-point overflow or underflow.

### 2. Covariance Matrix Regularization (Jitter)
When a GMM component collapses onto a single data point (or points lie in a low-dimensional subspace), the covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k$ becomes singular, causing its determinant $|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k| \to 0$ and its inverse $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k^{-1} \to \infty$.
To prevent numerical collapse, always add a tiny regularizing ridge ("jitter") to the diagonal:
$$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k + \epsilon \mathbf{I}, \quad \text{with } \epsilon \in [10^{-6}, 10^{-4}]$$

## Chapter 7 Toy Datasets

### Dataset 7A (1D Mixture of Gaussians)
- $\mathbf{X} = [-3.0, -2.5, -2.0, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0]^T$.
- Two distinct clusters: Component 1 centered at $\approx -2.5$, Component 2 centered at $\approx +2.5$.
- Useful for validating the E-step and M-step calculations by hand on paper before moving to 2D.

### Dataset H1 (Discrete HMM Weather & Activity)
A classic hand-computable Hidden Markov Model:
- **Hidden States**: $S = \{\text{Sunny} (0), \text{Rainy} (1)\}$.
- **Observations**: $V = \{\text{Walk} (0), \text{Shop} (1), \text{Clean} (2)\}$.
- **Initial Probabilities**: $\boldsymbol{\pi} = [0.6, 0.4]$.
- **Transition Matrix**:
  $$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.7 & 0.3 \\ 0.4 & 0.6 \end{bmatrix}$$
- **Emission Matrix**:
  $$\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 & 0.3 & 0.1 \\ 0.1 & 0.4 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}$$
- **Observation Sequence**: $\mathbf{O} = [\text{Walk} (0), \text{Shop} (1), \text{Clean} (2)]$.
- **Hand Check**: The most likely state sequence via Viterbi is $[\text{Sunny}, \text{Sunny}, \text{Rainy}]$.
