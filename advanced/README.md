# Chapter 7: Advanced Probabilistic Models

> **Hard Prerequisites (concepts, not chapters):**
> - **Probability theory:** Bayes' theorem, conditional probability, joint distributions
> - **Statistics:** expectation, variance, maximum likelihood estimation (MLE)
> - **Latent-variable models:** the concept that observed data is generated by hidden (unobserved) variables
> - **Iterative optimisation:** comfort with gradient descent or similar iterative parameter updates
>
> **Useful earlier work (recommended, not required):**
> - Chapter 4 (clustering) — K-Means is a special case of GMM; familiarity helps build intuition
> - Chapter 2 (classification) — Gaussian likelihood appears in Naive Bayes
> - Chapter 6 (neural networks) — builds optimisation maturity
>
> **Recommended roadmap position:** After Chapters 1–6. This chapter assumes strong mathematical maturity.

> **Scope:** Gaussian Mixture Models, the EM algorithm, and Hidden Markov Models only.
> No recommender systems, no CNN/RNN extensions.

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, you should be able to:

- Explain probabilistic generative models and how they differ from discriminative models
- Explain the Expectation-Maximisation (EM) algorithm as a general framework for latent-variable models
- Implement GMMs as a soft-assignment generalisation of K-Means
- Implement Hidden Markov Models and solve the three fundamental HMM problems

## Questions to Answer in Your Own Words

1. What is a latent variable and why are latent-variable models useful?
2. What does the EM algorithm do in the E-step vs the M-step? Why alternate?
3. Why does EM converge? (Hint: it monotonically increases a lower bound on the log-likelihood.)
4. How is a Gaussian Mixture Model related to K-Means? What does "soft assignment" mean?
5. What is the Markov assumption? What does a Hidden Markov Model capture that a simple Markov chain does not?
6. What are the three fundamental HMM problems — evaluation, decoding, and learning — and which algorithm solves each?

## Algorithms to Implement from Scratch

- [ ] Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with EM
- [ ] Hidden Markov Model (HMM) — Forward algorithm, Viterbi decoding, Baum-Welch (Forward-Backward) learning

## Mathematical Derivations to Complete

- Derive the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) from Jensen's inequality and show that maximising it maximises a lower bound on the log-likelihood
- Derive the GMM E-step (responsibility computation) and M-step (parameter updates for means, covariances, and mixing coefficients)
- Derive the Viterbi algorithm recurrence relation
- Show that K-Means is a special case of GMM with hard assignments and identity covariances

## Edge Cases & Tests to Consider

- GMM with K = 1: verify analytically that it reduces to fitting a single Gaussian (MLE for mean and covariance)
- Singularity: what happens when a Gaussian component collapses onto a single data point? (Covariance → 0, likelihood → ∞)
- HMM: test Viterbi decoding against brute-force enumeration on a small sequence (≤ 5 time steps)
- EM initialisation sensitivity: run GMM with different random seeds and compare results

## Completion Criteria

You are done with this chapter when you can:

- [ ] Fit a GMM to 2D data and visualise the learned Gaussian components
- [ ] Explain how GMM generalises K-Means (and demonstrate it on non-spherical clusters)
- [ ] Derive the EM update equations from scratch without notes
- [ ] Run Viterbi on a small HMM and verify the result by hand
- [ ] Explain when EM can get stuck in local optima and what to do about it
